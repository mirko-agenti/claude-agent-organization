# Structured Analysis

## Purpose

Apply a consistent analytical method across the AI organization. Produce work that is:

- structured;
- reproducible;
- transparent;
- traceable;
- evidence-based;
- decision-oriented;
- easy to review.

Use this framework alongside the relevant domain expertise. Do not treat it as a substitute for specialist knowledge.

## Operating Principles

- Address the stated objective and remain within scope.
- Use only authorized inputs and sources.
- Separate facts, assumptions, estimates, interpretations, and recommendations.
- Make uncertainty, limitations, and missing information explicit.
- Support conclusions and recommendations with the preceding analysis.
- Never fabricate data, evidence, sources, or certainty.
- Escalate when missing information prevents a reliable result.

## Analysis Workflow

### 1. Understand the Request

Identify:

- the objective;
- the expected deliverable;
- the decision to support;
- the available inputs;
- the constraints;
- the acceptance criteria;
- the relevant stakeholders.

Do not begin substantive analysis until the objective and expected output are sufficiently clear.

### 2. Define the Scope

State:

- the business or technical context;
- the included topics;
- the excluded topics;
- the relevant stakeholders;
- the dependencies;
- the applicable constraints.

### 3. Review the Evidence

Identify every authorized input used, including:

- documents;
- files;
- datasets;
- prior decisions;
- project memory;
- user-provided information.

Assess the quality of the evidence as high, medium, low, or unknown, and explain the classification.

### 4. Record Assumptions

For every material assumption, state:

- the assumption;
- why it is necessary;
- its confidence level;
- the effect on the analysis if it is incorrect.

Never present an assumption as a fact.

### 5. Perform the Analysis

Present a clear and verifiable analytical path:

- observations;
- relevant relationships;
- evaluation criteria;
- trade-offs;
- implications;
- supported findings.

Do not disclose hidden internal reasoning. Provide concise explanations, evidence, calculations, and decision-relevant rationale that a reviewer can verify.

### 6. Evaluate Alternatives

When alternatives exist, compare them using consistent criteria. For each alternative, assess:

- benefits;
- disadvantages;
- complexity;
- feasibility;
- risks;
- expected impact.

If only one viable alternative exists, explain why.

### 7. Assess Risks

For every material risk, specify:

- description;
- probability;
- impact;
- proposed mitigation;
- residual risk, when relevant.

Classify each risk as low, medium, high, or critical.

### 8. Form Recommendations

Derive recommendations directly from the evidence and analysis. For each recommendation:

- state the proposed action;
- explain its rationale;
- identify relevant dependencies;
- identify approval requirements;
- note material trade-offs.

Do not introduce unsupported recommendations.

### 9. Assess Confidence

Assign and justify one confidence level:

- High: evidence is sufficient, consistent, and verifiable.
- Medium: evidence is partially complete, but supports a reasonable conclusion.
- Low: important information is missing, ambiguous, or dependent on material assumptions.

## Information Classification

Use these labels consistently:

- Fact: information explicitly supported by an authorized input.
- Assumption: an unconfirmed premise required to proceed.
- Estimate: a derived value or forecast produced with a stated method.
- Interpretation: a reasoned explanation based on available evidence.
- Recommendation: a proposed action supported by the analysis.

## Missing Information

When information is missing:

1. Search the authorized project files.
2. Review the authorized project memory and recorded decisions.
3. Determine whether a clearly stated assumption is acceptable.
4. Evaluate the effect of the missing information.
5. State the resulting limitation.
6. Escalate if the missing information prevents a reliable result.

Never fabricate missing information.

## Quality Checklist

Before submitting the deliverable, verify that:

- the objective is addressed;
- the scope is respected;
- the inputs are identified;
- evidence quality is assessed;
- assumptions are explicit;
- facts and interpretations are separated;
- the analysis is complete and internally consistent;
- alternatives are evaluated when applicable;
- material risks are identified;
- recommendations follow from the analysis;
- the confidence level is justified;
- open questions are recorded;
- each acceptance criterion is verified;
- the result is saved in the required location.

## Definition of Done

The analysis is complete only when:

- the requested objective has been addressed;
- every required section is present;
- authorized inputs are traceable;
- assumptions and limitations are explicit;
- risks are documented;
- findings are supported by evidence;
- recommendations follow from the analysis;
- the confidence level is justified;
- unresolved questions are identified;
- acceptance criteria are checked;
- an independent reviewer can verify the result.
